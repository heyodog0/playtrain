## 2. SCOPED, NOT STARTED: Impoola + stride-2 encoder

Motivation: Table 1's encoder row costs 2.7x (0.94M Nature vs 0.35M IMPALA-CNN).
Measured on an H100, that gap is bigger than arithmetic predicts and the cause
is addressable. Already written up in the paper as Appendix E (`app:encoder`,
commit `40ae234`) — **as cost measurements only, no recommendation.**

### The measurements behind it (already done)

| encoder | MMACs | params | fwd+bwd @3072 | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | 8.13 | 1.13M | 2.48 ms | — |
| IMPALA-CNN | 30.61 | 0.62M | 24.78 ms | 1.00x |
| + stride-2 stem | 7.65 | — | 7.73 ms | **0.31x** |
| Impoola (GAP) | 30.09 | **0.11M** | ~= baseline | 0.98x |
| Impoola + stride-2 | 7.53 | 0.11M | — | ~0.31x |
| channels (8,16,16) | 8.22 | — | 20.97 ms | 0.85x |
| depthwise-separable | 12.69 | — | 25.70 ms | 1.04x |

**Only spatial reductions convert to throughput.** Narrow channels and
depthwise-separable convolutions cut arithmetic 3.7x and 2.4x and bought nothing
— not compute-bound at this scale. Do not spend time on them.

**Where the cost is:** stages 1-2 are 83% of the arithmetic, purely from running
at 64x64 and 32x32. **Where the capacity is:** 84% of parameters sit in the
dense layer after the flatten, which is 1.7% of the arithmetic.

### Implementation (~1 h)

- `ImpalaCNN` (playtrain-trainers `src/playtrain_trainers/policy.py`) gains
  `gap: bool` and `stem_stride: int`.
  - GAP: `self.fc = nn.Linear(depths[-1], features_dim)` and
    `x = x.mean(dim=(2,3))` instead of `x.flatten(1)`; the dummy-forward that
    computes `flat` disappears.
  - stride: `stage.conv = nn.Conv2d(c, d, 3, stem_stride, 1)` on stage 0 only.
- Register `impoola` / `impoola_s2` at **both** selection sites:
  `policy.py:168` (PPO) and `impala/net.py:128` (IMPALA).
- GAP makes the head resolution-independent, so the two changes compose cleanly
  — with flatten, stride-2 silently changes the FC input dim.

### Runs (~half a day wall, ~9 GPU-hours), gate stage 2 on stage 1

**Stage 1 — throughput**, 24-game suite x {impoola, impoola_s2}, ~1.8 h each,
parallel. Use `scripts/suite_icnn.sh` as the template (it is the headline
topology with only `net` changed). The real question is whether the encoder
microbenchmark's 3.2x survives inside the full trainer. It should — the
IMPALA-CNN suite is learner-bound and flat across games (190-197k at 1 learner)
— but verify before spending stage 2.

**Stage 2 — learning**, 8 games x 3 seeds x {impoola, impoola_s2} at 150M,
~2.8 h each. Baselines already exist (`impala_*` matrix, 3-seed IMPALA-CNN for
the panel-C games), so no third arm needed.

**What stage 2 can and cannot tell you:** it will catch catastrophic or roughly
neutral. It will NOT resolve 5-10% — ProcGen seed variance is too wide — and the
Impoola paper's +17% is about generalization to *held-out levels*, which the
current eval protocol does not measure.

### Risk

At 64x64 many sprites span only a few pixels (the notes record a key sprite at
~3x3 obs px). A stride-2 stem makes stage 0 see 16x16 instead of 32x32, which is
exactly the spatial detail IMPALA-CNN is preferred for on ProcGen. Could be
free; could cost real return. The two changes are separable, so a failure is
attributable.

### Literature (verified by search 2026-08-08)

- **Impoola** (arXiv 2503.05546): flatten -> global average pooling. +17%
  generalization on ProcGen, 35% fewer params.
- **Mind the GAP!** (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.17749): *independently* names the
  encoder-to-dense junction the limiting component and recommends GAP. Two
  papers converging on the same one-line change is the strongest signal here.
- **Higher Resolution, Better Generalization** (arXiv 2605.10546): Impala's
  flatten grows parameters quadratically with resolution; GAP decouples it; 28%
  gain at best resolutions. Relevant to PlayTrain: it renders at arbitrary
  resolution cheaply where ALE/ProcGen emit fixed frames.
- **ViT**: not competitive on RL sample efficiency without pretraining.
- Nobody is optimising encoder wall-clock. The stride-2 finding is not in the
  literature.

Both new refs are already in `iclr2027_conference.bib`
(`schmied2025impoola`, `obando2025gap`) and cited in Appendix E.

## 4. Cluster traps paid for on 2026-08-07/08

Recorded in `[[fasrc-benchmark-hazards]]`; the ones that cost the most:

- **Slurm time format.** `scontrol update TimeLimit=03:30` means **3m30s**, and
  it killed all 24 tasks of the first rerun attempt instantly. Use `HH:MM:SS` or
  `D-HH:MM`, and set walltime in the sbatch, not afterwards.
- **`native_env_threads: 0`** auto-sizes to the whole node: 4 ranks x 92 threads
  on 92 cores is a **26x** cliff (759,938 -> 29,740 env steps/s). Always set it.
  Under DDP, 12 threads/rank beat 23.
- **MPS**: missing daemon costs 1.9x and presents as a flat number across unlike
  games. Use `scripts/mps_warm.sh` (`mps_up`), which also pre-warms each GPU
  serially to dodge the multi-learner start-up race.
- **`nproc` reports 1** on these nodes because it honours `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`.
  That is not CPU starvation — check `sched_getaffinity`.
- **Never `uv sync`** against `analogen-jaxbench` (prunes hand-installed
  envpool/PyQt5 out from under concurrent jobs).
- **Node clocks vary 1.56x.** Exclude `holygpu8a134xx` for any paper number.
- **Push AND pull.** A cluster repo left un-pulled cost one rerun cycle
  (`--worker-offset` unrecognised, every fleet actor exited).
