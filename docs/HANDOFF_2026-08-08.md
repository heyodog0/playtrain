**Nature is 7.08 MMACs, not 8.13** — the published figure over-counts by exactly
one FC (4096x256 = 1.05M). Hand-check: 1.57 + 2.10 + 2.36 + 1.05 = 7.08. This is
in the paper; see §3.

**Only spatial reductions convert to throughput.** Narrow channels and
depthwise-separable convolutions cut arithmetic 3.7x and 2.4x and bought nothing
— not compute-bound at this scale. Do not spend time on them.

**Where the cost is:** stages 1-2 are 83% of the arithmetic, purely from running
at 64x64 and 32x32. **Where the capacity is:** 84% of parameters sit in the
dense layer after the flatten, which is 1.7% of the arithmetic.

### What "stride-2" actually is — five reframings, strongest first

**(a) It is not a cheaper encoder. It is a budget reallocation from space to
channels.** Cutting arithmetic 3.7x via channels bought 1.18x; cutting it 4.0x
via resolution bought 3.2x. `impoola_s2w` now confirms the converse: it spends
3.9x more arithmetic than `impoola_s2` (7.53 -> 29.21 MMACs) for only 1.5x more
time (7.75 -> 11.66 ms). **Width is nearly free; space is not.** So the honest
name is a *narrow-spatial, wide-channel* stem, and IMPALA-CNN sits on the wrong
side of that trade (16 channels at 32x32). `impoola_s2w` has the same arithmetic
as the baseline stem (29.21 vs 30.61) at 2.3x the speed and 2x the channels in
every stage — i.e. the reallocation is available at *higher* capacity, not just
lower cost. This is the version to prefer if stage 2 shows any regression.

**(b) Stem stride and render resolution are the same knob from opposite ends,
and PlayTrain is the only one of the three benchmarks that owns both.** The
entire mechanism is "everything downstream runs one octave lower." ALE and
ProcGen hand you a fixed raster; PlayTrain renders its own, and **`obs_size` is
already a config field** wired to the JS renderer — so `obs_size=32` is a
zero-code experiment that also cuts render cost and, critically, obs bytes on
the wire: **12,288 -> 3,072 per step.** The fleet was ruled out because the
fabric ceiling (10.3 GB/s / 12,288 B = 838k agent-steps/s) sat *below* the local
number (939k). At 32 px that ceiling is **3.35M, 3.6x local**. Quarter-size
observations resurrect the fleet, which is the headline-number consequence.
Two caveats: doing the resize *in-network*
(`set_obs_scale(0.5)`) costs 19.71 vs 15.43 ms fp32 — the interpolate kernel is
not free, so render natively rather than downsample in the net. And a strided
stem is strictly *more* informative than a low render (its 3x3 conv still
touches every 64 px pixel; a renderer discards them first) — unless the renderer
antialiases, which inverts that.

**(c) GAP buys no time; it buys bandwidth, twice.** 0.622M -> 0.106M is 5.9x
fewer params at 1.00x the time (25.27 -> 25.22 ms — genuinely free, not
"approximately"). But params sit on this system's critical path twice: the DDP
gradient all-reduce every learner step, and the learner->actor weight broadcast
every sync. GAP is the only change here that helps 4-rank DDP and the fleet
without touching the encoder's math.

**(d) GAP makes input resolution a runtime knob, so it can be scheduled.** With
flatten, resolution is welded into the FC (2048 vs 512 in-features). With GAP
the same weights accept 32, 64, or 96 px (tested). That legalizes **progressive
resizing**: half resolution for the first ~70% of frames, full for the rest —
0.7(0.31) + 0.3 = 0.52x, so ~1.9x end-to-end, with the *final* policy at full
resolution. That confines the tiny-sprite risk to early training, when the
policy is coarse anyway. `set_obs_scale` is deliberately a mutable attribute
rather than a constructor argument so a trainer can schedule it without
rebuilding the model or invalidating a checkpoint. **The schedule is not wired**
— that is a trainer decision and needs a learning run to justify.

**(e) What remains after stride-2 is depth, not space or width — and MPS
forfeits the fix.** At `impoola_s2` + scale 0.5 the encoder is 1.89 MMACs and
still 11.55 ms (fp32): 2.4x Nature's time on 0.27x Nature's arithmetic. That
floor is kernel launches — IMPALA-CNN issues 15 convs + 1 FC per forward, Nature
3 + 1. The standard fix is CUDA graphs, which `7119a57` **disables under MPS**
(capture and MPS are mutually exclusive). Real tension worth a footnote: MPS
buys 1.9x on the inference/env side and forfeits the fix for a launch-bound
encoder.

**Cheap insurance, only if needed:** if stage 2 regresses, the diagnosis is
aliasing (key sprites are ~3x3 px at 64) and the fix is blur-then-subsample
(Zhang 2019, BlurPool) — one fixed depthwise 3x3, which the sweep already showed
costs a launch rather than arithmetic here. Do not do it pre-emptively; doing it
*after* a regression is what makes the regression attributable.

### Predicted trainer-level effect (to be tested by stage 1, not yet measured)

The IMPALA-CNN learner spends ~68% of update time in conv (H100 kernel profile).
Amdahl on that share: `impoola_s2` -> 0.32 + 0.68(0.31) = 0.53, i.e. **~1.9x**,
so ~0.66M against IMPALA-CNN's 0.35M and Nature's 0.94M; `impoola_s2w` ->
0.62, **~1.6x**, ~0.57M. That would close roughly two-thirds of Table 1's
encoder gap. **This is a prediction from a microbenchmark and the whole point of
stage 1 is that microbenchmarks have not been trustworthy here** — the same
reasoning predicted gains from narrow channels that did not appear.

### If this becomes a workshop paper — do these in this order

1. **Fix the protocol first.** Training uses `formula` autoreset = the unbounded
   level distribution, so there is no train/test split and generalization
   **cannot be measured**. That is the currency this literature trades in
   (Impoola's +17%, Procgen-HD's 28% are both held-out-level results). The
   harness already supports `pool` mode — a fixed train pool plus held-out eval
   is a config change, and without it stage 2 can only ever report "not
   catastrophic."
2. **Add a plasticity axis, not a fourth encoder.** LayerNorm in the stem or
   periodic head resets. If `impoola_s2w` at 2x width is hitting an optimization
   ceiling rather than a capacity one, that is the most informative experiment
   available, and it reframes the result from "a cheaper encoder" to "cheap
   width, and here is what makes it usable."
3. **Progressive resizing is the novel bit.** GAP made resolution a runtime knob
   (`set_obs_scale`); scheduling it is standard in vision and absent in RL.
   ~1.9x end-to-end with the final policy at full resolution.
4. **Do not chase Hadamax or MoE** — both are sample-efficiency plays that cost
   wall-clock, the opposite of this argument. One sentence in related work so a
   reviewer does not think they were missed.

**The framing that holds up:** this is an engineering result — *here is the cost
structure of RL encoders, which nobody measured, and it is not what arithmetic
predicts*. Not "here is a better encoder." The honest comparison is parity with
IMPALA-CNN at 1.63x throughput, which is a good result that becomes a weak one
if oversold.

### Literature (searched 2026-08-08; the first pass was scoped too narrowly)

The first pass asked "what is a faster IMPALA-CNN" and so found only
IMPALA-CNN papers. Two corrections: there is a third live encoder, and the
field largely stopped competing on encoder architecture around 2019 — it is a
legacy default nobody re-optimised, which is exactly why the wall-clock finding
was available.

- **Hadamax** (arXiv 2505.15345): max-pooling Hadamard products between
  GELU-activated parallel hidden layers. 80% over vanilla PQN on Atari-57.
  **Missed by the first pass.** Performance, not wall-clock.
- **MoE unlocks parameter scaling** (arXiv 2402.08609): works on the IMPALA
  encoder specifically. **Sparsity unlocks scaling** (arXiv 2506.17204).
- **Plasticity-loss survey** (arXiv 2411.04832): 50+ mitigations. RL nets stay
  small because big ones do not *train*, not because they do not help.
- **Procgen-HD** (released with arXiv 2605.10546): a resolution-scaling
  benchmark already exists, so resolution-as-a-knob is taken. The unclaimed
  ground is the *cost* axis — resolution and width priced in wall-clock.
- **Impoola** (arXiv 2503.05546): flatten -> global average pooling. +17%
  generalization on ProcGen, 35% fewer params.
- **Mind the GAP!** (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.17749): *independently* names the
  encoder-to-dense junction the limiting component and recommends GAP. Two
  papers converging on the same one-line change is the strongest signal here.
- **Higher Resolution, Better Generalization** (arXiv 2605.10546): Impala's
  flatten grows parameters quadratically with resolution; GAP decouples it; 28%
  gain at best resolutions. Relevant to PlayTrain: it renders at arbitrary
  resolution cheaply where ALE/ProcGen emit fixed frames.
- **ViT**: not competitive on RL sample efficiency without pretraining.
- Nobody is optimising encoder wall-clock. The stride-2 finding is not in the
  literature.

Both new refs are already in `iclr2027_conference.bib`
(`schmied2025impoola`, `obando2025gap`) and cited in Appendix E.

## 4. Cluster traps paid for on 2026-08-07/08

Recorded in `[[fasrc-benchmark-hazards]]`; the ones that cost the most:

- **Slurm time format.** `scontrol update TimeLimit=03:30` means **3m30s**, and
  it killed all 24 tasks of the first rerun attempt instantly. Use `HH:MM:SS` or
  `D-HH:MM`, and set walltime in the sbatch, not afterwards.
- **`native_env_threads: 0`** auto-sizes to the whole node: 4 ranks x 92 threads
  on 92 cores is a **26x** cliff (759,938 -> 29,740 env steps/s). Always set it.
  Under DDP, 12 threads/rank beat 23.
- **MPS**: missing daemon costs 1.9x and presents as a flat number across unlike
  games. Use `scripts/mps_warm.sh` (`mps_up`), which also pre-warms each GPU
  serially to dodge the multi-learner start-up race.
- **`nproc` reports 1** on these nodes because it honours `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`.
  That is not CPU starvation — check `sched_getaffinity`.
- **Never `uv sync`** against `analogen-jaxbench` (prunes hand-installed
  envpool/PyQt5 out from under concurrent jobs).
- **Node clocks vary 1.56x.** Exclude `holygpu8a134xx` for any paper number.
- **Push AND pull.** A cluster repo left un-pulled cost one rerun cycle
  (`--worker-offset` unrecognised, every fleet actor exited).
