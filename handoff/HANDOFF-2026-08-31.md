## 2. Findings, in order of importance

1. **The published EnvPool arm was partly a driver artifact.** It pumped
   2,048 envs through ONE Python process (~25MB obs per step through one
   thread). EnvPool at PlayTrain's process topology — 16 pools of 128 envs x
   5 threads, STILL the sync API — is 3.9x faster than that arm on the same
   node. Fig 4A's orange curve understates EnvPool and cannot stand as-is;
   "EnvPool flattens ... which is remarkable" (line 541) must go.
2. **PlayTrain NUMA binding is worth ~1% on both node classes** (1.011x on
   the dense 96-core node, ~1.0x on the roomy one). Process isolation is an
   implicit NUMA strategy: per-worker first-touch already places every page
   locally, and there is no cross-process sharing to bind. "The default
   configuration is the fast configuration" is banked, with data.
3. **Why sync16 beats even EnvPool's async+numa mode** (mechanisms, for the
   appendix): (a) the async driver thread is memcpy-bound — 2 shards means
   ~8 GB/s of observation marshalling per Python thread, right at one core's
   memcpy ceiling; 16 drivers carry ~1.4 GB/s each; (b) dispatcher/queue
   contention scales with threads-per-pool (40 vs 5); (c) `+bind` helps
   EnvPool because its obs arena is first-touched by the constructor thread —
   PlayTrain has no equivalent to fix. Punchline: the best EnvPool
   configuration found is EnvPool rearranged into PlayTrain's shape —
   independent confirmation of the architecture thesis on the competitor's
   engine. Caveat: sync16-vs-async+numa is cross-node until 43246914.
4. **Table 1(b) is fair and untouched.** Its EnvPool arm already runs as 12
   per-worker pools (`vec_actor.py:385`), sync and single-buffered on both
   sides. Audited; no change needed.
5. **Topology sweep (43243581): the published 16w x 5t point is near-optimal
   at 80 threads** — best factorization (40w x 2t) is +3.4%. Whole-node
   92-thread configs are +17-18% but change the axis point; disclose
   separately if ever used. This lever is spent.
6. **Component profile** (job 43260288; genoa; live .so): interp
   (JS_CallInternal) 20-65%, rasterizer 8-34%, obs blit (render_obs_rgb,
   scalar RGBA->RGB) up to 25%, p5 bind 2-13%, **GC ~0%, alloc <=4%**. GC
   threshold and jemalloc levers are dead by measurement. FASRC has NO
   perf/gperftools; the working profiler is our LD_PRELOAD SIGPROF sampler:
   `playtrain-trainers/benchmarks/prof_preload.{c,so}`, `prof_symbolize.py`,
   driver `pt_prof2.sbatch`. py-spy cannot see the native env threads.
7. **rv2 (branch `native-tuning`) is the tuned PlayTrain build**: 1.291x
   ProcGen16 / 1.317x all-24 over the live binary at the published topology,
   obs-checksum bit-exact. Individually: SIMD blit 1.117, rasterizer
   target-cpu 1.046, PGO ~1.05, rust-PGO 1.046, hidden visibility 1.019.
   NG bump 0.15.1->0.16.2 is SAFE but null (0.998x). Full log:
   `handoff/tuning_notes.md` on the branch. **Adoption is an open user
   decision** — it invalidates every published PlayTrain number and costs a
   ~1-day re-measurement cascade + a 17402 confirm after 43246914 drains.
   Second open decision: portable build (l12, +19%, no PGO) vs tuned
   benchmark build (rv2) as the paper's artifact.
8. **Two pre-existing determinism holes on MAIN** (found by the tuning
   session's gates, present on the untouched base): `qbert` diverges from
   the V8 reference only on the terminal GAMEOVER frame; `aim_trainer`
   diverges from the reset frame. These touch the paper's bit-identical
   claim and predate all tuning. Needs its own investigation before
   submission.
